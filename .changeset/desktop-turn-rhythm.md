The conversation reads more calmly. Text now sits in a narrower column than the input box, so long lines no longer run from edge to edge of it. Your message has smaller corners and sits closer to the reply under it. Lists in a reply use small square bullets, and inline code is a tidier chip. The folded line about the work has its arrow after the words, and opening it shows the steps in one box with a thin edge, the action in grey and what it touched in the full text colour. While Namzu works, the line reads "Thinking" or the step it is on in one grey tone with four small dots that light in turn (they hold still if you reduce motion), and the plan is one box under your message instead of hiding inside the folded work; it folds into the work when the answer arrives. The counts on the result line are a size larger, and the Jump to latest button is a round button above the message box. Asking for permission is laid out afresh: a header band holds the question, the file's path has a row of its own, the change is a framed block with a solid bar on added lines and a hatched one on removed lines, and the buttons are smaller and further apart. Reject is a quiet grey button, not red text; the lasting yes has a small arrow that opens what it covers; and the keys that answer (Esc, Ctrl+Enter) are drawn on the buttons. The message box grows upward line by line with the plus and Send staying at its bottom corners, Send is a rounded square, Stop is a plain square instead of a red circle, a notice above the box is taller, and the three starters on the home screen are bordered rows under the heading. Numbered and bulleted lists in a reply start on the same line, a list inside an item sits close under it, a command too long for its row is shown once in full instead of twice, and a menu that is open while the window is resized or zoomed settles where it belongs instead of sometimes staying at the old size's place.
